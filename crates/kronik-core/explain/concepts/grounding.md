Grounding says how well a fork's effects rest on recordings. An effect is exact when causal key, ordinal, type and fingerprint match a recording; the recorded response is used. It is approximate when causal key, ordinal, type and target (method, host and route pattern) match but the request differs; the recorded response is reused, and the request difference is shown in the diff. It is synthetic when nothing matches: a stub rule answers, or `FailClosed` gives the guest `denied`, and the turn is marked divergent. The ordinal is the operation's position in one counter per turn, shared with `send`, `call`, `timers.schedule` and `connection.send-now`; a fork never pairs an effect with a recording at another ordinal. A call inserted, removed or moved before another therefore shifts the ordinals after it: graded against the recording at its new ordinal, it is approximate when that recording has the same target (exact when the requests are identical), and receives a response that can be another call's; otherwise it is synthetic. A call the fork no longer makes has no grounding row and does not lower confidence; the behavior diff's `effects` section reports it. Grounding is reported in total and per target, because thousands of exact calls can hide a few approximate payments, and policy judges it per target (ADR 0147).
